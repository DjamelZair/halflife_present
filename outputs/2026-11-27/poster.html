The ROAD Concept: A Mechanism-Guided Approach to Antimicrobial Therapy for Carbapenemase-Producing Bacteria.

Carbapenemase-producing Gram-negative bacteria are among the most challenging pathogens encountered in contemporary infectious diseases and critical care practice. Although antimicrobial susceptibility testing remains the cornerstone of laboratory diagnosis, reliance on the minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC) in isolation is insufficient to predict clinical outcome.

Highlights the importance of considering multiple factors beyond MIC for effective antimicrobial therapy.

Discusses pharmacokinetic/pharmacodynamic optimization strategies in critically ill patients.

Posaconazole in Clinical Practice: A Narrative Review of Current Evidence and Expert Recommendations.

Posaconazole is a broad-spectrum antifungal agent used to manage invasive fungal infections (IFIs), particularly in high-risk immunocompromised patients. It is active against a range of fungal pathogens, including Candida spp., Aspergillus spp., and Mucorales.

Strong evidence supports posaconazole as preferred agent for primary antifungal prophylaxis in high-risk patients.

Pharmacokinetics vary by formulation, with delayed-release tablets and intravenous formulations overcoming limitations of original oral suspension.

Study to Assess Rifaximin Soluble Solid Dispersion (SSD) for the Delay of Encephalopathy Decompensation in Cirrhosis

Study RNLC3131 is a Phase 3, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, multicenter study to assess the efficacy and safety of rifaximin SSD-40mg IR for the delay of the first episode of overt hepatic encephalopathy (OHE) decompensation in liver cirrhosis, defined by the presence of medically controlled ascites.

This study evaluates the efficacy and safety of a higher dose of rifaximin for delaying the first overt hepatic encephalopathy decompensation in liver cirrhosis patients with medically controlled ascites.

The study focuses on the efficacy of rifaximin SSD-40mg IR, potentially impacting its exposure and effect on decompensation delay.
