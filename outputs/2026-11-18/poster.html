Discovery of isoindolinone derivatives as potent, safe, and intravenously administrable NaV1.8 inhibitors for the treatment of pain.

The voltage-gated sodium channel NaV1.8 is primarily expressed in nociceptors in the dorsal root ganglia (DRG) and has emerged as a highly promising target for the development of non-addictive analgesics. Although the selective NaV1.8 inhibitor suzetrigine (VX-548) has demonstrated clinical efficacy, its poor physicochemical properties and significant CYP450 enzyme induction present challenges for intravenous administration and potential drug-drug interactions.

Compound 3c exhibits potent inhibition of the NaV1.8 ion channel, improved solubility, favorable pharmacokinetic properties, and reduced CYP450 enzyme induction.

Compound 3c has a half-life of 7.3 hours and clearance of 38.8 mL/min/kg.
