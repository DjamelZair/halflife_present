Interpreting antiretroviral exposure and drug-drug interactions in pregnancy: A clinically anchored framework for HIV pharmacology.

In pregnancy, antiretroviral drug-drug interactions must be interpreted against dynamic changes in gastrointestinal physiology, plasma-protein binding, renal elimination, hepatic enzyme activity and membrane transporter expression. Interaction studies quantify geometric mean exposure ratios relative to nonpregnant adults, but these measures describe relative pharmacokinetic change rather than whether effective exposure is preserved.

From CYP450 pharmacogenomics to personalized medicine: clinical evidence, dosing guidelines, and future perspectives.

Interindividual variability in drug response remains a critical challenge in contemporary pharmacotherapy, and cytochrome P450 (CYP450) genetic polymorphisms are a principal determinant of this heterogeneity. The CYP450 superfamily mediates the oxidative biotransformation of 70-80% of clinically prescribed medications.

03

Cytochrome P450 inhibitors: friends or foes?

Cytochrome P450 (CYP450) enzymes play a central role in the metabolism of xenobiotics and endogenous compounds, influencing drug pharmacokinetics, efficacy, and safety. Inhibition of these enzymes has traditionally been viewed as a major liability in drug discovery due to its association with drug-drug interactions (DDIs), toxicity, and metabolic instability.
