Pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic strategies to optimize antifungal therapy, prevent resistance and minimize treatment failure.

Invasive fungal diseases (IFDs) present significant challenges in clinical practice due to high morbidity and mortality rates, compounded by antifungal resistance, diagnostic limitations and pharmacokinetic (PK) and pharmacodynamic (PD) complexities. The main antifungal classes, triazoles, polyenes, echinocandins and pyrimidine analogues, are essential for managing IFDs but are increasingly undermined by resistance due to genetic mutations, efflux pump over-expression and biofilm formation.

Drug-Drug Interactions of Cenobamate With Medications: Dose-Dependent Induction/Inhibition and Clinical Implications.

Cenobamate causes dose-dependent cytochrome P450 (CYP)2B6 and CYP3A4 induction and CYP2C19 inhibition, raising concern over multi-pathway drug-drug interactions (DDIs) in patients with epilepsy treated with cardiovascular co-medications. We developed a physiologically based pharmacokinetic (PBPK) model for cenobamate and verified it using reported DDIs for cenobamate 200 mg using three probe substrates: bupropion (CYP2B6), midazolam (CYP3A4), and omeprazole (CYP2C19).
